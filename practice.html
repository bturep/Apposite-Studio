Practice

Services + process

Studio Apposite works primarily with existing buildings—projects where the most important design work is often integration: tying new work into old work, aligning new constraints with existing structure, and making repairs and upgrades legible rather than invisible.

We take on small projects (entries, stairs, built-ins, kitchens and bathrooms, decks, envelopes and upgrades) as well as larger renovations and additions. Where needed, we also produce documentation for permitting pathways and coordinate with consultants and builders.

Services

Studio Apposite provides architectural design and documentation services with an emphasis on existing houses and careful, design-sensitive alteration.

Typical services include measured site visits and drawing up existing conditions; conceptual studies and options; renovation and addition design; custom millwork and interior interventions; envelope and upgrade strategies; and drawing sets suitable for permitting and construction.

We also support projects that involve regulatory and documentation requirements—such as suite legalization pathways— by preparing clear drawing packages and coordinating information required for municipal review.

Approach

Our approach is grounded in measurement, proportion, and construction logic. We treat the existing house as material, evidence, and constraint—working with what is there rather than designing around an abstract ideal.

This often means making the “unseen” work explicit: structure, junctions, transitions, tolerances, and sequencing. The goal is not novelty, but fit—work that is buildable, legible, and continuous with what already exists.

Process

Most projects follow a phased structure. The names and boundaries can shift depending on scope, but the sequence is consistent: establish what exists, test options, develop a preferred direction, and then document it.

1. Existing conditions + constraints
Site visit, measurement, and a baseline drawing set; identification of key constraints (structure, code, zoning, services, access).

2. Concept options
A small set of design directions, studied through drawings and (when helpful) quick massing or spatial diagrams. We use this phase to decide what to pursue—and what not to pursue.

3. Design development
Refinement of the chosen direction: layout, junctions, key details, and coordination with consultants where required.

4. Documentation
Drawing set prepared for permitting and/or construction. Scope ranges from targeted sheets to full packages, depending on the project.

5. Construction support
Optional support during construction: answering RFIs, clarifying intent, reviewing changes, and maintaining continuity with the design.

Fees

Fees can be structured as a fixed package by phase, hourly, or a hybrid (fixed for early phases; hourly for open-ended support). The right structure depends on clarity of scope and the level of unknowns in the existing conditions.

For early-stage work, we often recommend a defined first phase (existing conditions + initial study) so that both parties can make an informed decision about the next steps.

If you’re unsure what you need, a short call is usually enough to propose a sensible first phase.
